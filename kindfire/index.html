Kindfire

Ten days as the artistic heart of London Climate Action Week, at The Building Centre, 26 Store St, London WC1E 7BT. Eighteen artists, six environmental urgencies, and an entire community that ate vegan for the duration. Self-financed, so every artist got the wall for nothing.

What it was

Rooted in the collective's mission to awaken consciousness about interconnected ecosystems and inspire meaningful action, Kindfire brought together artists working across waste, biomaterials, photography, sculpture, videomaking, performance and storytelling.

The show spanned six interconnected environmental urgencies, arranged so that visitors could feel how each issue touches every other. Art became the emotional connector, revealing the systemic links between our choices, our cultures and the living world.

Throughout the programme, artists and speakers offered accessible, tailored pathways for action, so that every visitor left with concrete steps they could take immediately. Kindfire positioned art as a catalyst for systemic change: kind, determined, universal and collective.

Hosted by The Building Centre, as the artistic heart of London Climate Action Week. Les Hydronautes, of BIUTOPIC, were among the eighteen; the Captain Paul Watson Foundation UK is a partner of the collective.

Six urgencies

  1. Energy Transition
  2. Overconsumption
  3. Food & the Kindness Revolution
  4. Climate Social Justice
  5. Interconnectedness
  6. How Climate Change Affects Me

The commitment

Everyone ate vegan. All ten days.

The team and every participating artist for the full duration of the event, and every guest speaker on the day they spoke. Not a gesture — a measurable subtraction, which we then measured.

Counted

Ten days, one kilogram at a time.

The team and every artist for the full run, every speaker on their day. Drag through the ten days and watch what did not happen.

One tile, one kilogram of CO₂. The basis: 44 people eating plant-based for the ten days, plus vegan refreshments for 150 guests at the private view and the finissage. Modelled by committee member Ascanio from the University of Oxford study in Nature Food (2023), with DEFRA and Poore & Nemecek (2018) factors. The drawing spreads the totals evenly over the days.
